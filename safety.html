Check it yourself

You should not have to take our word for it. This page shows where your Google password goes, what Class Ping can and cannot read, and how to check each claim yourself.

Where your password goes

  1. You and Google

    You type it on Google's page

    Your browser opens Google's own sign-in page. The address bar says accounts.google.com. Your password is typed there, and only there.

  2. Google asks you

    You choose what to allow

    Google lists what Class Ping wants to read. You tick the boxes and press Allow. You can say no.

  3. Google tells Class Ping

    It gets a pass, not a password

    Google gives Class Ping a permission to read your classes. Never your password. Class Ping has nothing to type it into.

Class Ping has no password box anywhere: not in the Windows app, not in the web app, not on this website.

What Class Ping can read

With Sign in with Google, these are the only things it asks Google for. All of them are read-only.

Your list of classesTo know which classes you are in, and to show each post with its class.
Announcements, assignments, questions and materialsTo show them, sort them, and tell you when something new is posted.
Your own classworkSo due dates show up on your to-do list.
The names of people who postTo show who posted each item.
Your email addressTo show which Google account is connected, and to tell accounts apart.

It cannot read your Gmail, your Drive, or anything outside Classroom, because Google never gives it that permission. With Sign in with Google and in the web app it can only read: it cannot post, edit or delete anything in Classroom. The optional Full features mode of the Windows app can post a comment, but only one you write and send yourself.

Check each claim yourself

  1. Look at the address bar. When Google's page opens to sign you in, the address should be accounts.google.com. That is where your password goes. If a page ever asks for your Google password and it is not Google's address, close it.
  2. Look for a password box. Open the web app, press F12, click Elements, press Ctrl+F and search for type="password". There is no result, because Class Ping has no such box.
  3. See where the web app talks. Press F12 and open the Network tab. You will see Google (to read your Classroom, plus pictures from Google and YouTube) and this website's own files. Nothing goes to a Class Ping server, because there is no Class Ping server.
  4. See what you have allowed. Go to myaccount.google.com/permissions, find Class Ping, and see exactly what it can read. You can remove it there at any time.
  5. Take your data back. In the Windows app, Settings, Sign out gives the permission back, and uninstalling deletes what it saved. In the web app, Settings, Sign out and erase does both.

What Class Ping does keep

The posts it read, which ones you ticked or moved, and the permission Google gave it. In the Windows app that permission is encrypted with Windows' own protection, so only your Windows account on that PC can use it. In the web app it lives only in that browser tab. With Full features, Chrome keeps a "stay signed in" record in a folder only Class Ping uses; Class Ping never sees your password in that mode either.

None of this is sent to us. The only thing that can reach us is feedback you choose to send. Every detail, including what an AI service receives if you turn that on, is in the privacy policy, and the rules for using Class Ping are in the terms. Something not clear? Ask us.
